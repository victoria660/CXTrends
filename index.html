Recommendations for Experience Improvement
1. Product Acceptance & Transition Support

Issue: 688 subscription cancellations and 332 refund requests stem from cats refusing food—over 1,000 conversations (13.9% of all contacts) driven by product acceptance failure.

Recommendations:

  • Proactive transition coaching: Send automated transition tips at days 3, 7, and 14 post-delivery with specific tactics (warming food, using toppers, gradual mixing ratios). Current conversations show agents offer these tips reactively after customers already want to cancel.
  • Early intervention: Flag customers who haven't reordered treats or made account portal visits within first 10 days; trigger outreach from Cat Concierge team before second box ships.
  • Save path refinement: When customers cite food acceptance, offer a one-time recipe swap or smaller trial of different proteins before processing cancellation. Current evidence shows most cancellations are processed immediately without save attempts.
  • Picky eater onboarding: Add a pre-purchase assessment for cats with known food sensitivities; set expectations about transition timeline and offer extended money-back guarantee window for these customers.
2. Subscription Transparency & Expectation Setting

Issue: 233 cancellations and 253 refund requests involve customers who didn't realize the trial automatically enrolled them in recurring shipments—486 conversations (6.6% of all contacts) driven by subscription surprise.

Recommendations:

  • Trial-to-subscription messaging: Make recurring nature of trial more prominent at checkout. Current evidence shows customers use language like "I only ordered samples" and "I never opted into subscription," indicating they missed or misunderstood the terms.
  • Pre-shipment control window: Send a prominent reminder 5-7 days before second box processes with clear "Skip This Order" and "Cancel Subscription" CTAs. Current Thursday 8pm cutoff is referenced in agent replies but may not be reaching customers proactively.
  • First-box invoice clarity: Add "Next scheduled order: [date]" and "Cancel anytime before [cutoff]" to trial order confirmation emails.
  • Self-serve cancellation: Make subscription cancellation available through account portal without requiring contact with support—reduce friction for customers who've made the decision and lower contact volume.
3. Shipping & Fulfillment Quality

Issue: 858 shipping contacts (+8.3% week-over-week), with carrier misdelivery (190 conversations), thawed/damaged deliveries (207), and wrong/missing items (106) as top drivers.

Recommendations:

  • Carrier performance review: Investigate carrier-specific patterns for misdeliveries and lost packages. Evidence shows customers receiving delivery photos of wrong addresses, suggesting carrier GPS or routing issues. Consider carrier diversification for problem zones.
  • Warehouse quality control: 106 conversations cite wrong or missing items—implement or strengthen pre-ship audits for order accuracy, particularly for multi-recipe orders where customers report receiving wrong proteins.
  • Thawed delivery prevention: 207 conversations involve food arriving thawed or warm. Review coolant pack quantities for high-temperature regions and extended transit routes; consider seasonal adjustments or expedited shipping for problem zones.
  • Proactive replacement protocol: When delivery photo doesn't match customer address, automatically trigger replacement order rather than requiring customer to request. Reduce time-to-resolution and anxiety for customers with senior or special-needs cats.
4. Product Availability & Inventory Management

Issue: Order modification contacts increased 21.5% week-over-week (+123 conversations), with recurring mentions of out-of-stock recipes forcing substitutions and creating friction in the customer experience.

Recommendations:

  • Inventory visibility: Provide customers with advance notice (7-10 days) when their preferred recipe will be out of stock for upcoming orders, with option to delay shipment or pre-approve substitution.
  • Substitution preferences: Allow customers to set backup recipe preferences in account settings so automatic substitutions align with known preferences rather than defaulting to generic alternatives.
  • Supply chain communication: When specific recipes face extended stockouts (e.g., Pulled Bird), send proactive email explaining issue and expected resolution timeline. Current evidence shows customers frustrated by repeated stockouts without explanation.
  • Flexible delivery scheduling: Simplify postponement/rescheduling workflow through account portal to reduce support contacts. Current process appears to require customer-initiated contact for many delay requests.

Recommendations for Improvement
1. Reduce Product-Fit Cancellations (543 subscription cancellations + 252 refund requests = 795 contacts)
Problem: 40.6% of subscription cancellations stem from cats refusing the food, despite transition tips. This is the single largest attrition driver.
Recommended Actions:
• Enhance the trial/sample experience: Offer a smaller, lower-commitment sample pack that doesn't auto-convert to subscription, or make the first box a true "trial" with explicit opt-in for continuation
• Improve transition guidance earlier: Send proactive transition tips *before* the first box arrives, not after customers report problems
• Expand recipe/texture options during onboarding: Use the initial quiz to surface picky eater patterns and recommend mixed textures or multiple proteins from the start
• Create a "picky eater" save path: When customers report cats won't eat, offer a one-time free small-portion variety pack to test alternatives before canceling
• Expected Impact: Even a 15-20% reduction in product-fit cancellations could save 80-100 subscriptions per week
2. Improve Subscription Model Clarity (244 cancellations + 161 unwanted orders + 202 billing disputes = 607 contacts)
Problem: 18.2% of cancellations involve customers who didn't understand the recurring nature of the service. Many feel "deceived" or describe the charge as "unauthorized."
Recommended Actions:
• Redesign checkout to emphasize subscription: Add an explicit "You are subscribing to recurring deliveries" confirmation step with frequency and next charge date displayed prominently
• Send a pre-processing reminder: Email customers 48-72 hours before the Thursday cutoff with order details, next charge date, and a one-click "Skip this order" button
• Clarify the cutoff in all change confirmations: When customers cancel, delay, or modify, show "This change takes effect after your [date] order" to set correct expectations
• Offer a one-time purchase option: For customers who explicitly want to "try before committing," provide a non-recurring first box at a slightly higher price point
• Expected Impact: Reducing "didn't know recurring" cancellations by 50% would save ~120 subscriptions per week and eliminate many billing disputes
3. Fix Thawed Delivery Issues (206 shipping contacts, 30% of shipping category)
Problem: Customers report food arriving partially or fully thawed, often with missing or insufficient ice packs, especially as weather warms. Safety concerns dominate the language ("I don't think it's safe").
Recommended Actions:
• Audit and adjust coolant algorithms immediately: Review packing procedures for April shipments to identify why ice packs are missing or insufficient during the seasonal transition to warmer weather
• Increase coolant proactively for high-risk zones: For regions forecasted above 75°F, automatically upgrade to higher ice pack counts without waiting for complaints
• Add delivery date flexibility: Allow customers to choose a specific delivery day or receive SMS alerts when the package is 2 stops away, reducing porch time in heat
• Provide post-thaw guidance upfront: Include a "What to do if your box arrives warm" card in every shipment with clear safety guidelines and immediate replacement request instructions
• Track repeat thaw incidents by carrier/route: 206 thawed deliveries in one week suggests patterns—identify problem carriers or routes and adjust shipping methods
• Expected Impact: Reducing thawed deliveries by 40% would eliminate ~80 weekly contacts and prevent negative word-of-mouth about product safety
4. Improve Cutoff Timing Transparency & Customer Control (167 "after processing" cancellation attempts + billing disputes)
Problem: Customers contact support to cancel or modify orders, only to learn they're past the Thursday 8PM EST cutoff. This creates a "too late" dead-end experience and drives billing disputes.
Recommended Actions:
• Surface cutoff timing everywhere: Display "Changes must be made by [Day/Time]" on the account portal, in emails, and in any order confirmation or upcoming delivery notice
• Add a processing countdown: Show "Your next order processes in 2 days" on the account dashboard with a prominent Skip/Edit button
• Extend the cutoff window or add a grace period: If operationally feasible, move the cutoff to Friday morning or allow same-day cancellations for orders not yet picked by the warehouse
• Offer a "cancel on next login" option: Let customers flag their account to cancel the next order if they don't log in by a certain date, giving them a safety net
• Empower agents with more flexibility: For customers who canceled just hours after processing, consider allowing agents to issue credits or partial refunds as a goodwill gesture
• Expected Impact: Reducing "too late" contacts by 30% would save ~50 weekly contacts and prevent escalations to chargebacks
5. Empower Agents with Flexible Refund/Credit Authority
Problem: Rigid "no refund" messaging on perishable goods creates frustration when customers believe they acted correctly to prevent a charge. Some threaten chargebacks, and agents admit they won't contest disputes.
Recommended Actions:
• Create clear exception criteria: Authorize agents to issue credits or partial refunds for: (1) orders placed within 24 hours of cutoff that customers attempted to cancel, (2) billing disputes with proof of prior cancellation attempt, (3) customers experiencing their first issue
• Offer account credit instead of cash refunds: For perishable goods policy compliance, provide store credit that preserves customer lifetime value while resolving the immediate frustration
• Test a "satisfaction guarantee" save: For first-time product-fit complaints, offer a one-time refund/credit if cats don't eat the food, converting a cancellation into a trial extension
• Track chargeback risk indicators: Flag conversations where customers mention bank disputes and escalate for immediate manager review with refund authority
• Expected Impact: Reducing chargeback threats and improving first-contact resolution for billing disputes would decrease negative reviews, protect payment processor relationships, and improve CSAT in the refund category
6. Reduce Contact Volume Through Proactive Communication & Self-Service
Problem: Many contacts stem from confusion about timing, next steps, or what's happening with their order/subscription.
Recommended Actions:
